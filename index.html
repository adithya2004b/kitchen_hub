Featured: Classic Margherita Pizza

A Neapolitan-style pizza with a blistered crust, bright tomato sauce, creamy mozzarella and fresh basil. The secret is a long, cold ferment and a very hot oven.

Ingredients

Method

  1. Mix flour, water, salt and yeast until no dry flour remains. Rest 30 minutes.
  2. Stretch and fold the dough four times over two hours, then refrigerate for 24–48 hours.
  3. Divide into four balls and bring to room temperature for 1–2 hours.
  4. Crush tomatoes by hand with a pinch of salt. Do not cook the sauce.
  5. Preheat oven and a baking steel to the maximum temperature for 45 minutes.
  6. Stretch dough, top lightly, and bake 6–8 minutes until leopard-spotted.
  7. Finish with basil and a drizzle of olive oil.

Breakfast Ideas

Fluffy Buttermilk Pancakes

Whisk 2 cups flour, 2 tbsp sugar, 2 tsp baking powder, ½ tsp baking soda and a pinch of salt. Combine 1¾ cups buttermilk, 2 eggs and 4 tbsp melted butter, then fold gently into the dry mix. Lumps are fine. Cook on a medium griddle until bubbles form, flip once.

Shakshuka

Sauté onion and red pepper in olive oil, add garlic, cumin, paprika and chili. Pour in crushed tomatoes and simmer 10 minutes. Make wells, crack in eggs, cover and cook until whites are set. Finish with feta and parsley.

Overnight Oats

Combine ½ cup rolled oats, ½ cup milk, ¼ cup yogurt, 1 tbsp chia seeds and a drizzle of maple syrup. Refrigerate overnight. Top with fruit and nuts.

Masala Omelette

Beat 3 eggs with chopped onion, tomato, green chili, coriander and turmeric. Cook in ghee over medium heat, fold and serve with toast.

Cooking Methods

Sautéing & Searing

Use a hot pan and dry food. Don't overcrowd — steam is the enemy of browning. Let proteins release naturally from the pan before flipping.

Roasting

High dry heat (200–230°C) caramelises sugars. Toss vegetables in oil, space them out and flip halfway.

Braising

Sear first, then cook slowly in a partial bath of liquid with a tight lid at 150°C. Tough cuts become tender and sauces gain body.

Poaching

Gently cook in liquid at 70–85°C. Ideal for eggs, fish and chicken breast.

Steaming

Preserves colour, nutrients and delicate textures. Keep water below the food and the lid snug.

Sous Vide

Vacuum-sealed food cooked in a precise water bath. Steak at 54°C yields perfect medium-rare edge to edge; finish with a hard sear.

Grilling & Smoking

Set up two zones: direct for searing, indirect for finishing. Smoke at 107–121°C for brisket and ribs.

Baking Science

Gluten forms when flour proteins meet water and are worked; bread wants lots, cakes want little. Maillard reaction begins around 140°C and gives crust its flavour. Leavening: baking soda needs acid; baking powder contains its own. Fat tenderises by coating gluten strands. Sugar attracts moisture and aids browning.

Always weigh ingredients. A cup of flour can vary by 30 g depending on how it's scooped, which is the single biggest cause of dry bakes.

Care & Maintenance

Seasoning cast iron

Wipe on a micro-thin layer of high smoke-point oil and bake upside-down at 230°C for an hour. Repeat for a slick, non-stick finish.

Sharpening knives

Use a whetstone at a 15–20° angle, starting at 1000 grit and finishing at 3000–6000. Hone on a steel before each use.

Descaling kettles & coffee machines

Run a 1:1 vinegar-water cycle monthly, then two rinse cycles with fresh water.

Wooden boards

Hand-wash only, dry upright, and oil monthly with food-grade mineral oil.

    Food Safety Basics

    Clean

    Wash hands for 20 seconds, sanitise boards and counters.

    Separate

    Keep raw meat away from ready-to-eat foods. Use different boards.

    Cook

    Reach safe internal temperatures; verify with a thermometer.

    Chill

    Refrigerate within two hours. Keep the fridge at 4°C or below.

    Danger zone: bacteria multiply fastest between 5°C and 60°C.
